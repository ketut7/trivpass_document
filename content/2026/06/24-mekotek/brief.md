## Caption

This Kuningan (27 June), the village of Munggu in Badung holds Mekotek — and it's worth planning around.

Hundreds of men raise long wooden poles into a moving cone, like a mountain, with the bravest climbing to the top. The name comes from the "tek-tek-tek" of clashing wood. It's believed to have begun as a victory rite in the 17th-century Mengwi Kingdom, and is now a prayer for protection, safety, and community wellbeing — recognised as an Intangible Cultural Heritage of Indonesia since 2016.

It begins in the morning after temple prayers and moves through the village. It's a public ritual, not a performance: keep clear of the poles, give families space, and photograph respectfully. Munggu is in Mengwi (Badung) — arrive early, the village gets busy quickly.

## Reference — Mekotek (Ngerebeg)

Source: web (kemdikbud.go.id/bpnbbali, detik.com, totalbali.com, sita.badungkab.go.id).

- Held every **Kuningan** day in **Munggu village, Badung** (this year 27 June 2026).
- Origin: **Mengwi Kingdom (~17th c.)** — a victory celebration for soldiers returning from battle against the Blambangan Kingdom (East Java).
- Men of the village lock **2–3.5 m wooden poles** (kayu *pulet*, tipped with a *tamiang* shield and pandan leaves) into a moving cone/pyramid (resembling a mountain); brave participants climb to the top. The name comes from the **"tek-tek-tek"** sound of clashing wood.
- On the morning, residents first gather at the village temple (Pura Desa) for a collective prayer.
- Meaning: a **prayer for safety and health**; villagers believe skipping it brings *gering* (disease/disaster).
- Recognised as an **Intangible Cultural Heritage of Indonesia** since 27 Oct 2016 (reg. 201600379).
