## Caption

Right now, Bali is in the middle of Galungan and Kuningan — its most important 10 days of the year, and in 2026 it happens only once.

Galungan (17 June) marks the victory of dharma over adharma, when ancestral spirits return to visit their families. You'll see penjor — tall, curved bamboo poles heavy with coconut leaf (janur) and offerings — arching over almost every street and home. Kuningan (27 June) closes the period: families pray to the gods and ancestors, offering nasi kuning (yellow rice) for prosperity in thanks, and asking for protection, safety, and guidance.

For travellers: expect penjor everywhere, quieter roads and some closed shops on the main days, and busy temples. Come as a respectful guest — dress modestly near temples, give processions space, and photograph politely.

Save this if you're in Bali this June.

## Reference — Kuningan

Source: provided by owner.

Kuningan honours Sang Hyang Widhi in the form of Sang Hyang Parama Wisesa — the holy spirits and dharma heroes who shaped noble human character. Hindus pray to the gods and ancestors, offering sesajen with ajengan (rice) coloured yellow; the yellow rice symbolises prosperity — a thanks for the blessings of prosperity in the world. The day before is **Penampahan Kuningan** (Sukra Wage Wuku Kuningan): livestock is slaughtered and offerings prepared for the next day. On Kuningan, offerings are made to the ancestors, asking for prosperity, protection, safety, and guidance before Ida Sang Hyang Widhi Wasa.
