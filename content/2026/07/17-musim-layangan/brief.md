## Caption

Every dry season, Bali's sky fills with giant kites — and it's a tradition, not just a pastime.

Flying a kite (layang-layang) is a sacred offering to Sang Hyang Rare Angon, the god of fields and wind, asking for a good harvest. They're built by groups and village banjar — from small kites to giants over 8 metres wide that take a whole team to launch: bebean shaped like a fish, janggan like a dragon with a cloth tail over 100 metres, and the leaf-shaped pecukan that only a master can fly.

Kite season runs June to September, peaking in July–August when the winds are strongest. You'll see them island-wide, and there are several free festivals around Sanur, Denpasar, and Gianyar — nothing to pay, nothing to book. Come in the morning for the biggest launches, and watch from the side: it's competitive and sacred at once.

Save this — or send us "kites" and we'll point you to the best beaches to watch.

## Reference — kite festivals 2026 (for caption / verify near the date)

- **Bali Kite Festival "Yadnya Cakra"** — Mertasari Beach, Sanur — ~18–19 Jul 2026.
- **Denpasar Kite Festival** — Sanur (Mertasari & Padang Galak) — August (largest, international).
- **Gianyar Kite Festival** — Gianyar coastline — August, free entry.
- Plus countless banjar/village competitions island-wide. Season: June–September (peak Jul–Aug). Source: Bali Travel Hub, Boutique Villas, How to Bali, Islands Events.
