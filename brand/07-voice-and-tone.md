# 07 · Voice & Tone

> How Trivpass speaks. Use this when you're writing anything — homepage hero, email subject line, deck slide, Instagram caption, press response, support reply.

---

## The voice in one sentence

**Trivpass sounds like a tech-savvy local friend who's been doing this for ten years and isn't going to oversell you anything — calm, specific, transparent, occasionally dry.**

---

## The four voice principles

### 1. Lead with the traveler's anxiety, not our story

Every piece of public copy answers one of the three traveler anxieties before it talks about us:

- *"Will I get scammed?"* — answered by driver-roster and price-clarity copy.
- *"Am I being overcharged?"* — answered by transparent itemized fees.
- *"Will the booking actually work?"* — answered by 24/7 ops and the driver intro email.

Bad:
> "Trivpass is Bali's leading travel agency, founded in 2026 by two locals passionate about authentic experiences."

Good:
> "Real drivers. Real prices. No surprises. Skip the scams and the haggling — tickets bundled with our internal driver fleet in one transparent checkout."

The traveler's anxiety is the lede. Our story is the supporting paragraph.

---

### 2. Be specific. Numbers, names, and details over adjectives.

Generic adjectives ("amazing", "premium", "authentic", "world-class") signal a brand trying to convince. Specific facts ("38-point vehicle inspection", "IDR 100,000 to the host village", "15-minute backup-driver SLA") signal a brand confident enough to show its working.

Bad:
> "Our drivers are extensively vetted and undergo a rigorous onboarding process to ensure the highest standards of safety and quality."

Good:
> "Every driver completes a 4–6 week onboarding: document review, 38-point vehicle inspection, CEFR B1 English assessment, and 30 supervised probation trips before joining the roster."

If you can't replace the adjective with a number, the adjective is doing nothing.

---

### 3. Itemize. The transparency promise is the layout, not just the copy.

This applies to:
- **Prices** — break down into driver, tickets, fuel, parking, taxes, community contribution, service fee. Always.
- **What's included** — list it; never bundle it under "everything you need."
- **What's not included** — list it too. Lunches at restaurant stops, tips, hotel pickup outside the standard zone.
- **Refund windows** — state the percentage at each time bucket. Don't hide it behind "see refund policy."

The receipt-style reservation card (see [08-visual-identity.md](08-visual-identity.md)) is the layout signature that makes this voice principle visible. The dot-leader fee rows are not decoration — they are the voice.

---

### 4. Don't sell what you can't ship. Avoid the lifestyle copy.

Trivpass does not write travel-blog poetry. We don't describe the sunrise at Mt. Batur as "breathtaking" or the Tegalalang rice terraces as "magical." We describe what you do, in order, with times and details.

Bad:
> "Imagine standing atop Mt. Batur as the sun paints the sky in shades of amber and gold, the volcanic mist swirling around you in this once-in-a-lifetime experience."

Good:
> "Pickup at 03:30. Two-hour climb at moderate pace. Sunrise from the summit at 06:15. Breakfast cooked on volcanic steam vents. Down by 09:00. Hot springs en route home."

The traveler can imagine the sunrise themselves. Our job is to tell them what time they need to be ready.

## The canonical CTA

The **primary call-to-action** across the product is:

> **"Secure Your Driver & Tickets"**

Never *"Book Now"*. Never *"Reserve"*. Never *"Get Started"*.

Acceptable variants for specific contexts:
- **"Confirm Booking"** — on the final checkout step.
- **"Reserve Your Spot"** — on cultural-event cards where capacity is the constraint.
- **"Continue"** — for multi-step forms.
- **"Add hotel address"** — for the post-confirmation upsell.
- **"View driver profile"** — on driver-roster surfaces.

The CTA discipline matters because *"Secure Your Driver & Tickets"* names two specific anxieties (will I get a real driver? will I have actual tickets in my email?) and answers them in the action word.

## Tone by context

The same voice modulates across contexts. Here's how the tone shifts.

### Marketing pages (homepage, attraction listing, cultural-event browse)

**Tone:** Confident, calm, specific. Slight dryness. No exclamation marks. No hyperbole.

> *"Real drivers. Real prices. No surprises."*
> *"All drivers are internal contractors — not random gig-app pickups. Background checks, vehicle inspections, language tests."*

### Booking & checkout (configure step, cart, payment)

**Tone:** Quiet, helpful, transactional. The voice steps back so the traveler can focus on the decision.

> *"You're booking the 06:00 pickup for 2 travelers · Vehicle: standard SUV · Pickup from Seminyak."*
> *"Total IDR 2,450,000 — including driver, tickets, fuel, parking, taxes, and one bottle of water per traveler."*

### Confirmation & day-of (confirmation email, driver intro email, T-48h reminders)

**Tone:** Operational, warm but efficient. The voice acknowledges that the traveler is about to actually do this.

> *"Your trip is confirmed. Here's everything you need."*
> *"Tomorrow's driver: Wayan Suarda · 12 yrs on the road · speaks English and Indonesian · vehicle DK 1234 AB (Toyota Innova). He'll be at your hotel reception at 05:50, 10 minutes before the 06:00 pickup."*

### Support replies & recovery (incident, refund, complaint)

**Tone:** Personal, owning, specific. Empathy first, then action. (See [06-trust-and-safety.md](06-trust-and-safety.md) recovery-message pattern.)

> *"That sounds awful, and we're sorry it happened."*
> *"We've issued the full refund — you should see it in 5–10 business days. The driver is off the roster as of this morning."*

### Cultural-event copy

**Tone:** Respectful, glossarial, careful. Use Balinese terms correctly. Acknowledge that the ceremony happens regardless of us.

> *"The Galungan Village Procession in Ubud is the morning walk during the ten-day stretch when Balinese ancestors return to earth. You'll walk the village paths at sunrise as the offerings are carried — attended as a respectful guest of the host *banjar*. IDR 100,000 of your booking goes directly to the community."*

### Press, partnerships, deck

**Tone:** Direct, confident, slightly more strategic. Lead with the moat argument, not the lifestyle pitch.

> *"Trivpass is a vertically-integrated travel agency for Bali. The model removes the broker layer between traveler and operator: drivers we vet and contract ourselves, ceremonies curated with the host village, prices stated all-in. The defensibility is the operating model, not the software."*

## Microcopy do's and don'ts

### Do say

- *"Verified driver"* (specific, defensible)
- *"All-in price"* (the brand promise)
- *"From IDR …"* (always preceded by "from" when display is range-based)
- *"Cancellation: 100% before 48h · 50% 24–48h · 0% under 24h"* (state the policy inline)
- *"IDR 100,000 to the host community"* (transparency about contribution)
- *"Limited to 12 visitors per session"* (capacity is real, not invented urgency)
- *"24/7 ops in Seminyak"* (location-specific)

### Don't say

- ❌ *"Book now"* — use *"Secure Your Driver & Tickets"*.
- ❌ *"Premium"* / *"Luxury"* / *"World-class"* — generic; we are not these things.
- ❌ *"Authentic"* — overused. Show the authenticity through specifics.
- ❌ *"Hidden gem"* — cliché.
- ❌ *"Bucket list"* — cliché.
- ❌ *"Once-in-a-lifetime"* — pressure language.
- ❌ *"Don't miss out!"* / *"Limited time only!"* — manufactured urgency.
- ❌ *"#1 in Bali"* — unprovable, OTA-coded.
- ❌ *"Hassle-free"* — overused, vague.
- ❌ *"We take care of everything"* — too soft; replace with specifics about what we do.
- ❌ *"Banjar approved"* / *"Village endorsed"* — overclaims relationships.
- ❌ *"Ethical"* / *"Sustainable"* / *"Responsible"* — claim by demonstration, not by adjective.
- ❌ *"Founded by passionate locals"* — every Bali brand says this. We don't need to.

## House style

- **Numbers:** Use digits for everything in product surfaces (prices, times, durations, traveler counts). Spell out in editorial prose only when starting a sentence.
- **Currency:** Always uppercase IDR/USD/EUR. Prices render in Inter weight 600 Terracotta in the product UI — see [08-visual-identity.md](08-visual-identity.md).
- **Time:** 24-hour format in product (`06:00`, not `6 AM`). Editorial prose can use `6am` (lowercase, no space).
- **Dates:** Long form in marketing (*"Saturday, 15 June"*), short form in product (`15 Jun 2026`).
- **Bali-specific terms:** Italicized on first use, plain text after. *odalan*, *purnama*, *banjar*, *Pemangku*, *canang sari*, *Galungan*, *Kuningan*, *Nyepi*, *Melasti*, *Mekotek*. Hyphenated proper nouns (Ogoh-Ogoh, Omed-Omedan) stay capitalized and hyphenated.
- **Capitalization of product types:** *Tour Package*, *Cultural Event*, *Attraction*, *Custom Trip* are capitalized as product categories. The same word lowercase (*tour packages we offer*, *the attraction is open until*) is fine in prose.
- **Sentences:** Short. Two clauses max in marketing copy. Run-on sentences signal trying too hard.
- **Em dashes** for asides — sparingly. **Semicolons** are fine; they tighten a sentence without breaking it.
- **No exclamation marks** in product copy. One per page maximum in editorial copy, and only when genuinely warranted.
- **No emoji** in product copy. Acceptable in social channels with extreme restraint (one per post max).

## Quick reference — three good headlines and why

**Homepage hero:**
> *Real drivers. Real prices. No surprises.*

*Why it works:* three short clauses, three of the brand's core promises, no adjectives. The traveler can read it in 1.5 seconds and know what we are.

**Cultural-events landing:**
> *Real ceremonies. Attended as guests, not audiences.*

*Why it works:* names the anti-pattern (audience at a staged show) by inversion. Doesn't claim authenticity; demonstrates the distinction.

**Custom trip builder:**
> *Tell us what you want. Our AI builds three drafts. You book the one that works.*

*Why it works:* describes the mechanism transparently. The AI is acknowledged. The user is in control. No marketing magic, just the actual workflow.

## When the voice has to shift (and how)

Three rare contexts where Trivpass voice changes shape:

### 1. Apology / incident recovery

Drop the brand-marketing register entirely. Be a human being, in plain language, acknowledging what happened. The voice is *softer, more personal, more specific* — but still no legalese.

### 2. Regulatory / legal copy

(Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, Tax notice.) Use plain-English plain-language phrasing inside the legal section. Avoid "shall", "heretofore", "the Company" — use "we", "you", and active voice. Indonesian PT registration details go in the footer band, not the body copy.

### 3. Indonesian-language copy

When we translate to Bahasa Indonesia, the voice stays calm and specific but the formality is slightly higher — Indonesian-speakers expect *bapak/ibu* honorifics on the first interaction. Casual register (*kamu*) is acceptable on social channels but not in transactional email. Our translation workflow: founder writes EN → AI draft for ID/ZH/KO → founder review → publish.

## How to test if your copy is on-voice

A short checklist:

- ☐ Does it answer one of the three traveler anxieties before talking about us?
- ☐ Is there a number, name, or specific detail in the first 30 words?
- ☐ Could I delete every adjective and still have a meaningful sentence?
- ☐ Did I use any banned phrases ("authentic", "hidden gem", "once-in-a-lifetime")?
- ☐ Is the CTA "Secure Your Driver & Tickets" or a sanctioned variant?
- ☐ Have I itemized anything that should be itemized?
- ☐ If this is a price, did I use the Inter 600 Terracotta treatment with the *"From"* prefix where appropriate?

If you can check all seven, the copy is on-voice.
