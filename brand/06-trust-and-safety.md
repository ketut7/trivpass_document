# 06 · Trust & Safety

> The full safety story — what we do, what we promise, and how we describe it. Trust is the brand's load-bearing claim; it has to be defensible at the detail level.

---

## The five layers of safety

```
1. Before they ever drive you   → Driver vetting + onboarding
2. Before each trip             → Vehicle inspection, dashcam, insurance
3. Night before pickup          → Driver intro email (photo, plate, phone, languages)
4. During the trip              → 24/7 ops line, driver phone, guide phone, GPS tracking
5. If something goes wrong      → Anti-scam guarantee, 100% no-show refund, driver review
```

Each layer is a separate promise. Together they cover the whole arc of a traveler's relationship with Trivpass, from first browse to post-trip review.

---

## Layer 1 — Driver vetting & onboarding

Every Trivpass driver is an internal contractor — not a freelancer found via WhatsApp, not a gig-app pickup, not a broker-sourced operator. We vet, train, and contract each driver directly.

A driver joins the roster only after a documented **4–6 week onboarding**:

| Step | Duration | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Document review | Days 1–3 | SIM (driver's license), KTP (national ID), SKCK (police clearance), references |
| 2. Vehicle inspection | Week 1 | 38-point check by Trivpass mechanics |
| 3. English & cultural assessment | Week 2 | Conversational interview / English road test |
| 4. Code-of-conduct training | Weeks 2–3 | No commission stops, no shop deals, anti-scam, first aid |
| 5. Probation period | Weeks 3–6 | Supervised trips; first several followed up with traveler courtesy call |
| 6. Roster confirmation | Ongoing | 90-day re-inspections, regular conduct reviews, 24-month SKCK refresh |

**Performance bar after onboarding:**
- Rolling rating threshold. Drivers below it go into formal review.
- Single confirmed scam report = permanent off-board.
- 90-day vehicle re-inspection. Regular conduct review. 24-month police-clearance refresh.

### How to describe it

- ✅ *"Every driver completes a documented 4–6 week onboarding before joining the roster — including a 38-point vehicle inspection and a police-clearance check."*
- ✅ *"All drivers are internal contractors — not random gig-app pickups."*
- ✅ *"Background checks, vehicle inspections, language tests."*
- ✅ *"Single confirmed scam report = permanent off-board."*
- ❌ *"All drivers are the best in Bali."* — unprovable.
- ❌ *"Drivers handpicked by our founders."* — they're vetted by a process, not a personality.
- ❌ *"Salaried drivers"* / *"Salaried roster"* — current copy framing avoids the salary claim. Use *"internal contractors"* / *"directly contracted"* / *"drivers we vet ourselves"* instead.

## Layer 2 — Vehicle & insurance

- **38-point inspection** before a vehicle joins the roster.
- **90-day re-inspection cycle** for every vehicle on the roster.
- **Dashcam in every vehicle** — for incident investigation, not for tourist surveillance.
- **Comprehensive insurance held by Trivpass** — covers vehicle, driver, and passengers. Single insurance relationship, single claim line. Travelers are *not* asked to insure separately.
- **First-aid kit + emergency contacts in every vehicle.**

### How to describe it

- ✅ *"Every vehicle on a 90-day inspection cycle. Comprehensive insurance held by Trivpass — passengers are covered."*
- ✅ *"Dashcam in every vehicle for incident investigation."*
- ❌ *"Brand-new vehicles."* — most are 2–5 years old; we don't claim brand-new.
- ❌ *"You don't need travel insurance."* — we don't substitute for personal travel insurance; we add vehicular coverage.

## Layer 3 — Driver intro email

The night before every pickup (around 19:00 WITA), the traveler receives an email with:

- Driver's **name** and **photo**.
- Vehicle **plate number** and **model**.
- Driver's **phone number** (call/WhatsApp).
- **Languages spoken** by the driver.
- Pickup **time** and **hotel address** (if confirmed; otherwise reminder to confirm).
- Trivpass ops **phone number** as backup.

This is the email that exists because travelers should never feel uncertain about who is picking them up. They can verify the plate before getting in the car.

### How to describe it

- ✅ *"The night before your trip, you get a full driver intro: name, photo, plate, phone, languages. You can verify everything before you get in the car."*
- ❌ *"You'll never feel lost."* — soft, generic. Replace with specifics.

## Layer 4 — During-the-trip channels

While the traveler is on a trip, three contact paths exist:

| Channel | When to use |
|---|---|
| **Driver's phone / WhatsApp** | First-line — running late, change of plans, "we want to skip stop 3" |
| **Guide's phone** (if a guide is on this trip) | Cultural-event interpretation, ritual questions |
| **Trivpass 24/7 ops line** (Seminyak office) | Anything driver-related ("driver didn't show", "feels off", "we want a different driver"), safety incidents, billing |

**GPS tracking** is active on every vehicle. Ops can see where the driver is in real time — used for backup-dispatch decisions, not for traveler surveillance.

**Backup driver dispatch within 15 minutes** if a driver doesn't show. The 15-minute window is the published SLA.

### How to describe it

- ✅ *"Three contact paths every trip: driver, guide, and Trivpass ops in Seminyak — 24 hours a day."*
- ✅ *"If a driver doesn't show within 15 minutes, we send a backup and refund the trip."*
- ❌ *"Always reachable."* — vague. Use the SLA instead.

## Layer 5 — Anti-scam guarantee & refund mechanics

### Refund windows

| Time before pickup | Refund |
|---|---|
| > 48 h | 100% |
| 24 – 48 h | 50% |
| < 24 h | 0% |
| Driver no-show | **100% — Trivpass absorbs the cost** |
| Cultural event cancelled by host *banjar* | **100% automatic refund** |
| Anti-scam claim (verified) | **100% refund** |

### Anti-scam guarantee

If a traveler credibly reports any of these on a Trivpass trip, we issue a full refund:

- Commission stop (driver detoured to a shop he gets paid to bring tourists to).
- Cash pressure ("the price you booked doesn't include X").
- Off-itinerary detour without consent.
- Vehicle unsafe / driver intoxicated / aggressive behavior.
- Overcharge / no-show.

The reporter's **privacy is protected** — we don't disclose driver-internal actions to the reporter.

A single confirmed scam report results in **permanent off-board** for the driver. We have a one-strike policy on this — there is no second chance for commission stops or cash pressure.

### Incident workflow

1. Traveler submits report via `/anti-scam` or by calling `safety@trivpass.com` / the 24/7 line.
2. Acknowledgment email sent within hours.
3. Triage by ops (read description, contact reporter for clarification, review GPS + dashcam if available).
4. Resolution: dismiss / warn driver / refund / off-board driver.
5. Reporter receives outcome email (sanitized — we don't disclose driver-side discipline).

### How to describe it

- ✅ *"100% automatic refund if a driver no-shows. 100% on cultural-event cancellations. 100% on a verified anti-scam report."*
- ✅ *"Single confirmed scam report = permanent off-board. We have a one-strike policy on commission stops."*
- ❌ *"Zero tolerance."* — overused phrase. Use the specific consequence instead.

## What we do *not* claim

A short list of safety claims we explicitly avoid, even though they sound good:

- ❌ **"100% safe."** Nothing is 100% safe. We say what we do, not what's impossible.
- ❌ **"Government-approved."** We are a registered Indonesian company (PT) but we don't claim special government endorsement.
- ❌ **"Police-partnered."** No formal partnership. Our drivers carry SKCK; that's it.
- ❌ **"AI-monitored trips."** GPS tracking is automation, not AI. Use the right word.
- ❌ **"Best safety record in Bali."** Unprovable; everyone claims it.

## When something does go wrong

This is the most important section of this document.

When a trip has a real problem — a driver no-shows, an accident happens, a traveler is scammed — the brand's voice has to lead with **empathy, then ownership, then action**. Never legalese, never deflection.

### The shape of a recovery message

```
[Empathy — name the experience]
"That sounds awful, and we're sorry it happened."

[Ownership — name our responsibility]
"This is on us. The driver was on our roster, and our job is to vet that."

[Action — what's already happening, what comes next]
"We've issued the full refund — you should see it in 5–10 business days.
The driver's off the roster as of this morning. Our ops lead, Made, will call
you tomorrow with the full incident summary."

[Open door — explicit invitation to keep talking]
"If anything else comes up, reply to this email or call us at +62-xxx-xxx-xxx.
We mean it — keep us in the loop."
```

### Things to avoid in recovery messages

- ❌ "We understand your frustration." → use *"That sounds awful"*. Less corporate.
- ❌ "Per our terms…" → don't lead with policy. Lead with people.
- ❌ "We will look into this." → use specifics: who, by when, what action.
- ❌ "This is not a typical Trivpass experience." → defensive. Say *"This is on us"* instead.

The recovery experience is when the brand promise actually has to deliver. A traveler whose first booking ends in a no-show but whose recovery feels personal will book again. A traveler whose first booking is fine but whose support reply is corporate will not.
