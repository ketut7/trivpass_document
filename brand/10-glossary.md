# 10 · Glossary

> The terms Trivpass uses, how to spell them, and what they mean. Lean on this when writing about Bali — the cultural-respect bar is set by getting the terminology right.

---

## Bali / Indonesian terms

These are the words that come up most often in Trivpass copy. Italicized on first use in any piece, plain text thereafter.

### *Banjar*

The Balinese village council — the unit of community governance. Bali has thousands of *banjars*; each one is a self-organizing community body of a few hundred families that owns a temple, organizes ceremonies, and decides what visitors are welcome at which events.

**Usage:** *"… attended as a respectful guest of the host *banjar*."*
**Capitalization:** lowercase in running text. Capitalize when prefixed with a place name: *"the Banjar of Pengosekan"*.
**Plural:** *banjars* (English plural), not *banjar-banjar* (which is grammatically correct in Indonesian but reads as foreign-language code-switching in English copy).

### *Pemangku*

A Balinese temple priest. The *Pemangku* runs the ceremony, leads the prayer sequence, and is the person who decides whether visitors are welcome.

**Usage:** *"… the *Pemangku* will explain the ritual sequence before the procession begins."*
**Capitalization:** Capitalized as a role title.
**Plural:** *Pemangku* (uncountable in English context — *"three Pemangku led the procession"* not *"three Pemangkus"*).

### *Odalan*

The temple anniversary ceremony. Every Balinese temple has its own *odalan*, held every 210 days according to the Bali Hindu calendar (the *pawukon* cycle). The *odalan* is the biggest event in that temple's year.

**Usage:** *"… we have one *odalan* on the calendar this month, at the Pura Dalem in Ubud."*

### *Purnama*

Full-moon day. *Purnama* prayers happen at temples across Bali on every full moon — a smaller, more frequent ritual than the *odalan*.

### *Galungan*

The ten-day festival when Balinese ancestors return to earth. Falls every 210 days. The single most significant religious holiday on the Bali Hindu calendar. The **Galungan Village Procession** in Ubud is one of the seeded cultural-event examples.

**Usage:** *"… the morning of Galungan, the village paths are lined with *penjor* (decorated bamboo poles)."*
**Capitalization:** Always capitalized (proper noun, like Christmas or Diwali).

### *Kuningan*

The closing day of the *Galungan* festival, ten days after *Galungan* begins. Ancestors return to the afterlife at noon on Kuningan. The **Mekotek** ritual in Munggu village happens on every Kuningan.

**Capitalization:** Always capitalized.

### *Nyepi*

The Balinese Day of Silence — the Saka New Year. No traffic, no flights, no lights, no work for 24 hours. The events on either side of Nyepi (Melasti before, Ogoh-Ogoh on the eve, Omed-Omedan after) anchor the most distinctive week on Bali's calendar.

**Capitalization:** Always capitalized.

### *Ogoh-Ogoh*

The giant demon effigies, built by each *banjar* over weeks, paraded through the streets on the evening before Nyepi and then ceremonially burned. The most visually distinctive night of the Balinese year. Hyphenated and capitalized.

**Usage:** *"… the Ogoh-Ogoh parade is on the eve of Nyepi — every village builds one, and they burn at midnight."*

### *Omed-Omedan*

The annual "kissing ritual" in Sesetan village (Denpasar), held the day after Nyepi. Unmarried youth from the village pair off in a centuries-old tradition unique to Sesetan. Hyphenated and capitalized.

### *Melasti*

The pre-Nyepi sea-purification procession. Four days before Nyepi, every *banjar* in Bali parades its sacred objects to the nearest beach for ritual cleansing. Photogenic from a respectful distance, no host permission needed for spectators.

**Capitalization:** Always capitalized.

### *Mekotek*

The wood-pyramid ritual unique to Munggu village (Mengwi), held on every Kuningan day. Men interlock 2-meter wooden poles into a swaying pyramid that another man climbs — visually distinctive and recognised cultural heritage.

**Capitalization:** Always capitalized.

### *Odalan*

The temple anniversary ceremony. Every Balinese temple has its own *odalan*, held every 210 days according to the Bali Hindu calendar (the *pawukon* cycle). The most significant *odalan* on the calendar is **Odalan Besakih** at Bali's mother temple on the slopes of Mount Agung.

**Usage:** *"… we have one *odalan* on the calendar this month, at Pura Dalem in Ubud."*

### Pesta Kesenian Bali (PKB)

The annual Bali Arts Festival — month-long, government-organized, running since 1979. Held in Denpasar from mid-June to mid-July. Dance, music, craft, and food across multiple venues. Public, ticketed, family-appropriate.

**Capitalization:** Always capitalized as a proper noun. The acronym PKB is acceptable on second reference.

### *Canang sari*

The small daily offering tray — a square of woven palm leaf filled with rice, flowers, and incense — placed at thresholds, dashboards, and shrines across Bali every day. The single most ubiquitous visual element in lived Bali. (Marigold petals are commonly part of the offering — the same warm color family as Trivpass's Terracotta accent.)

**Usage:** *"… *canang sari* on every doorstep before sunrise."*

### *Penjor*

The decorated bamboo pole, 5–8 meters tall, erected outside houses for *Galungan*. Symbolizes Mount Agung and prosperity.

### *Pura*

A Balinese Hindu temple. Often used as a place-name prefix: *Pura Besakih* (the mother temple), *Pura Dalem* (death temple), *Pura Ulun Danu* (lake temple at Bratan).

**Capitalization:** Capitalized as part of the temple name.

### *Sarong + sash*

Standard temple-entry dress for visitors. The sarong is the wrapped lower garment; the sash is the cloth tied around the waist over the sarong. Trivpass provides both for every cultural-event booking — non-negotiable, included in the price.

**Usage:** *"… sarong and sash provided at every cultural-event booking."*

## Trivpass product terms

These are internal product names. Use them consistently across surfaces.

### Tour Package

A pre-built day trip with a fixed itinerary, fixed stops, and a fixed all-in price. Capitalized as a product category in product UI; lowercase in editorial prose.

### Cultural Event

A curated access to a real Bali ceremony, attended as an invited guest of the host *banjar*. Capitalized as a product category. **Aspirational in v1** — the partner network is still being built.

### Activities & Attractions

The umbrella product category for single experiences bookable as standalone with optional driver. Two internal subtypes (`experience_type` in the data model):

- **Attraction** — a single site with timed entry (Tirta Empul, Uluwatu, Tanah Lot, Tegalalang, Lempuyang).
- **Activity** — a bookable experience with a duration and operator (Waterbom water park, Bali Zoo, Tanjung Benoa watersports, ATV rides, white-water rafting, hot-springs day passes, day clubs).

Always plural and ampersand-joined in customer-facing copy: *"Activities & Attractions"*. Singular forms (*"Activity"*, *"Attraction"*) are fine as card-level eyebrow labels.

### Custom Trip

The AI-generated multi-day itinerary, built from a traveler-submitted brief. Capitalized as a product category.

### Custom Trip Brief

The form the traveler submits — dates, traveler count, vibes, pace, regions, budget, must-visit, notes, contact. Capitalized when referred to as a noun (*"the Custom Trip Brief"*), lowercase as a verb (*"submit a custom-trip brief"*).

### Booking

The traveler's confirmed purchase. One Booking can contain one Booking Item (in v1; multi-item bookings are scaffolded for future). Each Booking has a public reference like `TVP-2026-Q5K7N4`.

### Booking Item

A single experience within a Booking — one tour package, one cultural event, one activity or attraction, or one day-segment of a Custom Trip. Capitalized in product context.

### Booking Ref

The short public identifier for a Booking. Format: `TVP-<year>-<6 chars Crockford base32>`. Example: `TVP-2026-Q5K7N4`. Used in the URL, in the confirmation email subject, and in the reservation card header.

### Driver Intro Email

The email sent the night before a trip, containing the driver's name, photo, plate, phone, and languages. The operational embodiment of the brand's safety promise. (See [06-trust-and-safety.md](06-trust-and-safety.md) Layer 3.)

### Anti-Scam Report

A traveler's submission via `/anti-scam` describing a problem on a Trivpass trip — commission stop, cash pressure, off-itinerary detour, etc. Each report becomes an *Incident* in the admin system.

### Incident

The internal record of an anti-scam report, safety event, or driver-conduct issue. Lifecycle: `new → under_review → resolved_<outcome>`. Each Incident has a public reference like `INC-2026-04231`.

### Brief Reference

The public identifier for a Custom Trip Brief. Format: `BRIEF-<year>-<5 chars>`. Example: `BRIEF-2026-W4271`.

## Operational terms (engineer-facing, occasionally CEO-facing)

### Dispatch

The internal system that matches drivers to bookings. Runs as a nightly cron at 18:00 WITA, assigning drivers to next-day pickups by region, language, vehicle type, and rotation.

### Roster

The total active Trivpass driver fleet. *"The roster"* in conversation = all current drivers; *"the driver roster"* in marketing copy = same.

### Onboarding

The 4–6 week process a new driver goes through before joining the roster. (See [06-trust-and-safety.md](06-trust-and-safety.md) Layer 1.)

### Probation

The 30-supervised-trip period at the end of onboarding. A driver on probation has their first 5 trips followed up with a courtesy call to the traveler.

### Off-board

The verb for permanently removing a driver from the roster. Used after a confirmed scam report or a sustained pattern of low ratings. *"The driver was off-boarded on Thursday."*

### WITA

Indonesia Central Time (Waktu Indonesia Tengah). UTC+8. Bali is in this time zone. No DST. All Trivpass operational times are expressed in WITA in user-facing copy. (UTC in the database.)

## Phrases that are part of the brand canon

These are sanctioned, often-repeated phrases that recur across surfaces. Treat them as canonical — don't paraphrase.

### *"Secure Your Driver & Tickets"*

The canonical primary CTA. Never *"Book Now"*. (See [07-voice-and-tone.md](07-voice-and-tone.md).)

### *"Real drivers. Real prices. No surprises."*

The homepage hero. Three short clauses, three brand promises.

### *"The real Bali, uncomplicated."*

The footer tagline.

### *"The number you see is the number you pay."*

The transparent-pricing one-liner. Used in pricing-explainer copy and the about page.

### *"100% automatic refund if a driver no-shows."*

The strongest sentence in the refund-policy block. Quoted verbatim on the safety page and the refund-policy page.

### *"Attended as guests, not audiences."*

The cultural-events distinction one-liner. Names the anti-pattern (staged tourist show) by inversion.

### *"Drivers we vet ourselves."* / *"All drivers are internal contractors."*

The driver-roster framing — used in place of any "salaried" claim. Specific driver-count numbers are not currently used in production copy.

### *"From IDR …"*

The price prefix on listing cards (when the price is a starting figure). Always *"From"*, never *"Starting at"*.

## Phrases we explicitly don't use

A short list of phrases that drift in and need to be caught at edit time:

- ❌ *"Book now"* — use *"Secure Your Driver & Tickets"*.
- ❌ *"Platform"* — we are an agency, not a platform.
- ❌ *"Marketplace"* — same reason.
- ❌ *"Premium" / "Luxury" / "World-class"* — generic, unprovable.
- ❌ *"Authentic"* — overused. Demonstrate authenticity through specifics.
- ❌ *"Hidden gem" / "Bucket list" / "Once-in-a-lifetime"* — clichés.
- ❌ *"Don't miss out!" / "Limited time only!"* — manufactured urgency.
- ❌ *"Banjar approved" / "Village endorsed" / "Pemangku certified"* — overclaims relationships.
- ❌ *"Best in Bali" / "#1 in Bali"* — unprovable, OTA-coded.
- ❌ *"Hassle-free"* — overused, vague.
- ❌ *"Passionate locals" / "Local insiders"* — every Bali brand says this.

## Pronunciation guide (for podcast appearances, video voiceovers, press calls)

- **Trivpass** — *TRIV-pass* (rhymes with "give-pass"). Not *TREVE-pass*, not *TRIPE-ass*.
- **Banjar** — *BAHN-jar*. Hard "j", like in "jam."
- **Pemangku** — *peh-MAHNG-koo*. Stress on the second syllable.
- **Odalan** — *oh-DAH-lahn*. Stress on the second syllable.
- **Purnama** — *poor-NAH-mah*.
- **Galungan** — *gah-LOONG-ahn*.
- **Kuningan** — *koo-NEENG-ahn*.
- **Nyepi** — *NYEH-pee*. The "ny" is a single nasal sound, like in *canyon*.
- **Ogoh-Ogoh** — *OH-goh OH-goh*. Repeat with equal stress.
- **Omed-Omedan** — *oh-MED oh-MED-ahn*.
- **Melasti** — *meh-LAHS-tee*.
- **Mekotek** — *meh-KOH-tek*.
- **Besakih** — *beh-SAH-kee*. (The temple is *Pura Besakih*.)
- **Pesta Kesenian Bali / PKB** — *PES-tah keh-seh-nee-AHN BAH-lee*. The acronym is read English-style: *"pee kay bee"*.
- **Pura** — *POOR-ah*.
- **Canang sari** — *chah-NAHNG SAH-ree*.
- **Penjor** — *PEN-jor*.
