# 04 · Target Audience

> Who Trivpass is for — and who it is deliberately not for. Audience clarity is the single biggest input to copy that converts.

---

## The core audience in one line

**International travelers visiting Bali for 5–14 days who want a real, well-organized trip without scams or staged tourism — and are willing to pay a fair price for the agency to handle it.**

---

## The three primary personas

### Persona 1 — "The Couple's Anniversary Trip"

> *Marcus & Anna, 38 & 36 · Sydney · 9-day trip · ~AUD 5,500 trip budget*

**What they look like:** Mid-career professionals, traveled internationally before but never to Bali. Booking the trip together, splitting the planning. Anna runs the Pinterest board; Marcus runs the spreadsheet. Both work full-time and have limited bandwidth for trip-planning evenings.

**What they fear:**
- *"What if the driver tries to scam us?"* — they've read the r/bali threads.
- *"What if we pay $200 and the ceremony turns out to be a tourist show?"*
- *"What if I book the wrong tour and Marcus is bored?"*
- *"What if the website charges extra at the end?"*

**What they want:**
- A trip that *just works*. Driver shows up, ceremony is real, ticket is in their email.
- A few "wow" moments (one big cultural event, one stunning sunrise hike).
- Time to also do nothing — they're not trying to fill every day.
- One transparent invoice they can expense, share with each other, or screenshot back home.

**Why Trivpass for them:** Removes the comparison-shopping and the worry that one of the three OTAs they're comparing is hiding a fee. Lets them book the trip in 90 minutes and stop thinking about it.

**What they buy:** 2–3 tour packages + 1 cultural event + 1 activity or attraction. Custom trip builder is their first stop; they end up booking the AI's nature angle.

---

### Persona 2 — "The Family with Kids"

> *The Henderson family — Dave, Priya, two kids ages 7 and 11 · London · 11-day trip · ~£6,000 trip budget*

**What they look like:** Two parents, two school-age kids, second trip to Asia. The kids' patience for "another temple" is finite. The parents want some real Bali for themselves but are calibrating every choice against "will the kids melt down."

**What they fear:**
- *"What if the driver doesn't have car seats?"*
- *"What if the tour is too long and the kids hate the rest of the day?"*
- *"What if the day's plan falls apart and we're stuck on a back road with two tired kids?"*
- *"What if we book something that's not appropriate for kids?"*

**What they want:**
- Drivers who are comfortable with kids (and have the language to talk to them).
- Trips with built-in flex — short enough that an unhappy kid doesn't sink the day.
- One agency to call if the day's schedule needs to change at noon.
- Pricing that includes everything, because the last thing they want is the driver asking for extra parking money in front of the 7-year-old.

**Why Trivpass for them:** The 24/7 ops line. The fact that there's a human in Seminyak who can re-route the day. The transparent pricing that means no checkout surprises with the kids watching.

**What they buy:** 3–4 activities & attractions (often water parks, wildlife parks, family-friendly day-clubs) + 1 short tour package + a custom trip with the kids in mind. Often skip the cultural events (most are 12+ age caps).

---

### Persona 3 — "The Solo Repeat Traveler"

> *Jenny, 31 · Toronto · 7-day return trip · ~CAD 3,000 trip budget*

**What they look like:** Has been to Bali once before, on a backpacker budget. This time has a real job and is back to do the trip "properly" — maybe a yoga retreat, maybe a couple of cultural days, maybe staying at the better hotel this time. Knows enough about Bali to be picky.

**What they fear:**
- *"What if I book the touristy version and miss the real thing?"*
- *"What if I get scammed because I think I know better?"* — repeat travelers are over-confident and a frequent target.
- *"What if I waste a day on a tour that's beneath my level of Bali familiarity?"*

**What they want:**
- Real cultural access — the thing she missed last time.
- A driver who can recommend the spots that aren't on the obvious list.
- Pricing that respects her ability to compare — she'll know if we're inflated.
- Flexibility to do half-days and self-direct the rest.

**Why Trivpass for them:** The cultural-event line is built for her. The driver roster is the way she gets past the "tourist version" of Bali. Transparent pricing builds trust with someone who's hard to fool.

**What they buy:** 1–2 cultural events (the highest-margin product), 2 activities or attractions, 1 custom-trip half-day, often no tour package.

## Secondary audiences (don't optimize for, but don't lose)

- **Wellness travelers** booking a yoga retreat and wanting cultural add-ons.
- **Honeymooners** — same shape as the Couple's Anniversary Trip but slightly higher budget.
- **Friend groups (3–5 people)** — typically a bachelor/bachelorette weekend extension. Convertible to tour packages, mostly skip cultural events.
- **Digital nomads** in their second month in Canggu wanting to "finally do the cultural stuff." Convert well on cultural events and activities & attractions; rarely book tour packages.
- **Group bookings (corporate retreats, school trips, friend trips of 10+).** Coordinated billing, multi-vehicle dispatch, sometimes a single contact handling everything. We do serve these — the ops side knows how to coordinate multi-car days and consolidated invoices. Expect a higher-touch booking flow (often direct email to `hello@` or `trips@`) rather than self-serve checkout for very large groups.
- **The photo-spot traveler.** People who are coming to Bali partly *for* the Tegalalang swing, the Lempuyang gates, the Handara archway, the Wanagiri tree-nest. Our Activities & Attractions catalog includes an *Adventure & Photo Spots* category for exactly this — we sell official tickets and a driver who knows the queue, the light, and the entry order. We don't manufacture or stage the spots; we just make booking them honest.

---

## Who Trivpass is *not* for

It's just as important to name the people we shouldn't optimize for:

- **The shoestring backpacker.** Trivpass is not a budget option. Hostels, Grab, and DIY work fine for them; we'd be over-priced for the value they want.
- **The luxury concierge client** ($1000+/day). We are not custom-bespoke at that price point. They should hire a private trip designer.
- **The "Bali expat" buying for their cousin's visit.** They typically already have a driver and don't need Trivpass — and they'll over-index on the cheapest option because they're spending someone else's money.

Saying "no" to these segments is what lets us be sharp for the three primary personas.

## How audience picture changes the copy

For the **Couple**, the headline answers *"will I get scammed?"* and the price-clarity story matters most.

For the **Family**, the headline answers *"what if something goes wrong?"* and the 24/7-ops story matters most.

For the **Solo Repeat Traveler**, the headline answers *"is this real Bali?"* and the cultural-events story matters most.

The current homepage leads with the Couple's framing because that segment is the largest by transaction count. The cultural-event landing page leads with the Solo Repeat Traveler framing. The about page and the safety page lead with the Family framing. The activities-and-attractions catalog page leads with the price-and-tiers framing, since most of that audience arrives with a specific experience already in mind.
